How Timezones works

One base timezone, and any number of other places. Every other place shows two things: its live current time and the converted time for the date & time you picked in the base zone. It's a world clock, and it's a meeting planner — “what time is it in Tokyo when it's 3pm in London?” — answered at a glance.

1 The layout

Everything lives in one column: the base search on top, the source of truth in the middle with the date & time you convert just under it, your places in a card grid below. The numbers point at each piece.

2 Anatomy of a card

Each location card carries the same nine parts (the base card swaps 2–3 for its accent frame and shows no relative chip).

3 How a conversion works

A worked example with today's dates: you pick Sun 4 Oct 2026, 13:30 in Sydney. Every card resolves the same instant through UTC, then into its own zone — DST and all.

Your 13:30 in Sydney is 02:30 UTC is 22:30 the previous evening in New York — the day shifted, so the card earns its −1 day badge — is 03:30 the same morning in London. The app does exactly this for every card using the browser's IANA timezone database: DST, half-hour offsets, and historical transitions are all handled natively. The amber DST chip on each date line tells you when a zone is on its summer clock.

4 The chips, decoded

Small pills, each with one job:

America/New_York
Zone name

The IANA timezone behind a city. Only shown for real zones — a fixed offset like +05:30 has no zone name to show.

UTC+05:30 fixed · no DST
UTC offset

The zone's current offset from UTC. For IANA zones it flips when DST starts or ends; a fixed offset never does and carries the fixed · no DST note.

Sydney, Australia −10:00
Relative to base

How far this place is from the base right now: + = ahead, − = behind. It flips automatically when either zone's DST changes. Hidden for grid cards whose base sits on a zero offset (the number would just duplicate the UTC chip).

+1 day −1 day
Day shift

On the converted row only: the local date of your picked moment differs from the base date, so the badge makes the jump explicit.

DST
Daylight saving

On the date lines (live and converted, base and all cards): that zone is on its summer clock at that instant. Fixed offsets and UTC never get one.

Base
Base tag

Marks the source of truth. The base card also wears the accent frame, and has no × and no drag — it's pinned.

5 Controls & gestures

Everything you can do, at a glance:

Calendar icon

Opens the native date picker; your pick is written back as YYYY-MM-DD. Typing in the field works too (slashes accepted, normalised on commit).

Now

Both fields jump to the base's current date & time — then you can nudge either one.

12h / 24h toggle

Reformats every time on the page (default 24h). Your choice is saved.

Theme

Auto by default — follows your OS/browser dark mode, live. Click for a fixed choice; it sticks until you click again or Reset.

Searching

Type a city, IANA zone, or offset. ↑ ↓ + Enter pick a suggestion, Esc dismisses. A highlighted suggestion beats raw text on Enter.

Reorder by drag

Drag a card (or its ⠿ grip); the rest reflow around it and the new order is saved. Drop outside the grid to cancel. The base card is pinned.

Remove ×

Takes one place away. The same place can't be added twice, but two cities in one zone can coexist.

Reset

In the footer: throws away your setup and returns to first-run — your local zone as base, a few popular cities, 24h, auto theme.

Export / Import

In the footer. Export downloads your whole setup as a JSON file; Import restores it. Your backup against clearing the browser's data — see below.

6 What the app remembers

Everything you touch — base, places, card order, date & time, 12/24h, theme — is saved the moment it changes, in your browser's localStorage. There's no account and no server: your setup lives in this browser, and once the page has loaded it works fully offline. (This page and the app share the same settings — your theme choice follows you here.)

Because it lives only in this browser, the app carries its own backup: Export in the footer downloads your whole setup as a small JSON file (timezones.json) — base, places in their on-screen order, the selected date & time, 12/24h, and theme. If you ever clear the browser's storage, Import that file to get everything back.

When you want a clean slate, Reset in the footer restores the first-run defaults.

7 Common questions

The things people usually wonder:

+Why is the date a text field instead of a normal date picker?

A visible native date input renders in your OS locale's format — DD/MM/YYYY in the UK, MM/DD/YYYY in the US — and can't be pinned to one. So the field is text in a fixed YYYY-MM-DD, with the native picker behind it: the calendar icon opens your browser's own calendar, and whatever you pick is written back as YYYY-MM-DD. Typing still works exactly as before.

+I added Mumbai and New Delhi — their clocks look identical. Bug?

Not a bug: both cities are in the same IANA zone, Asia/Kolkata. Same zone, two different places — both are kept, each removable on its own, and the zone chip shows they share a timezone. Adding the same place twice is what gets rejected (with a brief red flash).

+What if the time I pick falls on a daylight-saving transition?

The app resolves it without complaining: a time in a spring-forward gap snaps to the post-transition instant; an ambiguous fall-back time resolves to the later occurrence. The conversion is always a real instant — the rule is applied, not surfaced as an error.

+Can I use half-hour offsets?

Yes — +5:30, +5:45, +9:30 all parse (Kathmandu, Sri Lanka, Darwin). They render as fixed offsets with no DST, since a number can't carry DST rules; pick the real zone instead if you want that.

+Do the clocks show seconds?

No — live clocks are hour:minute and refresh exactly on the minute boundary, so nothing jitters and digits never shift. And none of this needs a network: the page is self-contained and works offline.

+I cleared my browser data — how do I get my setup back?

If you'd Exported first, Import that JSON file from the footer and everything is restored — base, places in their order, date & time, 12/24h, and theme. Without a backup file there's nothing to restore: there is no server copy, which is exactly why the backup exists.
